2. Checking the method: four random samples of 300 papers

Papers holding a real dataset: glioblastoma 19.6% · breast 18.8% · colorectal 19.4% · leukemia 17.2%. All four together: 202 of 1,076 papers (18.8%); 156 (14.5%) as spreadsheets.

The four cancers agree within three points; the spreadsheet rate (14.5%) matches the full corpus (14.9%). We re-examined 90 of the classifier's decisions file by file, with the AI assistant, not blind; verdicts are published. All 60 files it called datasets were real data tables; every table it missed was under 50 rows. On 500 random papers, 15% mention a repository the text-mining missed.

4. Rescued data put back to work

  • Two rescued brain tumor tables from different papers join on 244 shared patients (over 96% agreement). Pooled to 1,037 patients, they reproduce the known IDH survival difference (validation, not a new finding).
  • Sample patient tables: ~4,400 patients, ~500 from hospital cohorts not in TCGA, GEO or dbGaP.
  • Existing systems checked: BioStudies, publisher Figshare deposits, NLM FAIR-SMART, NLM Dataset Catalog. None sorts datasets by content, counts by funder, or reaches NCI's index.

5. Built and ready

  • Catalogue file: all 9,543 datasets in the Index of NCI Studies' exact 27-column format (deliverable/). Eight also packaged with DataCite metadata for deposit.
  • Working tool: attachment-rescue.vercel.app/app — search, look up any paper or NCI grant, open the file.
  • Code and data (MIT): github.com/OJ-IRO/attachment-rescue
